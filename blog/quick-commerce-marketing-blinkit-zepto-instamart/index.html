Quick Commerce Marketing in India: How to Grow on Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart

By the Mediamozak Team22 Oct 20264 min read

Ten-minute delivery apps have changed how people in Indian cities buy groceries, snacks, personal care and even electronics. For brands, Blinkit, Zepto and Swiggy Instamart are now a sales channel in their own right, with their own rules. Getting listed is only the first step. Here is how to actually grow there.

How quick commerce is different from marketplaces

On Amazon or Flipkart, your product can sit in a large warehouse and ship anywhere. Quick commerce platforms deliver from small "dark stores" close to customers. Each dark store stocks only a limited range, so platforms behave more like a modern retail chain than an open marketplace:

  • Shelf space is limited. Products that don't sell steadily lose their place.
  • Availability is local. Your product only shows to customers whose nearby dark store has it in stock.
  • Decisions happen fast. Shoppers scroll quickly and buy on impulse, often adding items to a basket they already planned.

Step 1: Choose the right products to launch

Don't list your full range on day one. Start with a few products that:

  • Fit an everyday or impulse need (snacks, beverages, personal care, pantry, small gifts).
  • Have a price that fits a typical quick commerce basket.
  • Have healthy margins after platform commissions, discounts and ad spend.
  • Are compact and robust enough for fast picking and delivery.

Make sure your paperwork is ready before applying: GST registration, FSSAI licence for food products, trademark or brand authorisation, barcodes and correct MRP labelling.

Step 2: Build listings that win the scroll

Customers decide in a second or two, usually on a phone screen. Your listing needs:

  • A clear main image: product front-facing on a clean background, with the pack size readable.
  • A searchable title: brand + product + key variant + size, for example "Brand Name Roasted Makhana – Peri Peri, 80 g".
  • Supporting images: benefits, ingredients, usage, and size comparison.
  • The right category and keywords so you appear when people search generic terms like "healthy snacks" or "protein bar", not just your brand name.

Step 3: Use platform ads wisely

All three platforms offer advertising, typically sponsored search placements, category banners and promotional slots. A sensible approach:

  1. Protect your brand searches so competitors don't appear when people search your name.
  2. Bid on high-intent category keywords where your product is a strong fit, and track sales per keyword.
  3. Time campaigns to demand: mornings for breakfast items, evenings for snacks, match nights for beverages, and festivals like Diwali and Raksha Bandhan for gifting.
  4. Cut what doesn't convert. Review return on ad spend weekly, not monthly. Quick commerce moves fast.

Step 4: Never go out of stock

Stock-outs are the silent killer in quick commerce. If a dark store runs out, your product disappears for nearby customers, your ads stop working there and your sales history weakens. Work closely with the platform's category team on forecasts, plan extra stock before festivals and sale days, and track availability by city and dark store.

Step 5: Pick platforms and cities strategically

Each platform has different strengths in different cities, and their footprints change quickly. Rather than launching everywhere, many brands:

  • Start in one or two metros where their target customers live, such as Delhi NCR, Mumbai or Bengaluru.
  • Prove sales velocity on one platform.
  • Expand to the next platform and city once supply and margins are stable.

Step 6: Drive demand from outside the app

Quick commerce works best when people already know your brand. Use Instagram Reels, influencer collaborations and local Meta campaigns with "Now available on Blinkit / Zepto / Instamart" messaging. These raise branded searches inside the apps, which usually convert better and cost less than generic keywords.

Frequently asked questions

How can a new brand get listed on Blinkit or Zepto?

Brands usually apply through the platform's seller or brand partner page and go through category review. Having GST, FSSAI (for food), proper packaging and labelling, and a clear plan for supply makes approval more likely.

Is quick commerce profitable for small brands?

It can be, but only with careful pricing. Commissions, discounts, ads and logistics add up, so calculate your contribution margin per order before scaling ad spend.

Which categories sell best on quick commerce?

Everyday and impulse categories such as snacks, beverages, dairy, personal care, household essentials and small gifts tend to perform best, though platforms keep expanding into electronics, beauty and more.
